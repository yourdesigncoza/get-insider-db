Branded Checkout


==================

When you are purchasing and item and it asks you how you would like to pay, if you see a button with the PayPal or Venmo logo. That is their branded checkout.

I am focusing on this segment for two reasons:

1.  It is 30% of their total payment volume but more than 50% of their net transaction profits in 2025.

2.  It has been under the most pressure.


Since 2024 growth here has decelerated down to 1% in their Q4 earnings and 4% for the year.

[

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2oRk!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe75105fd-19d3-4aa6-98a9-6a3ea539ea76_3072x1600.png)

This deceleration was attributed to weakness in the consumer, slower rollout of new features leading to less adoption, and competition.

I believe the key attractions when customers choose their checkout method are low friction and high trust.

So let’s see how consumers are voting with their dollars by comparing against competition in 2025.

Keep in mind PayPal’s branded checkout TPV grew 4%, total TPV grew 7%, BNPL TPV 21%, and Venmo revenue around 20% in 2025.

_Competition: Apple Pay, Google Pay, Amazon Pay, Shop Pay, Klarna, Affirm_

1.  **Apple Pay**: It is very hard to find concrete data but all Apple Pay transaction volume grew 21% YoY in 2025. This includes both online and point of service payments, tap to pay. That part is growing faster than 21% so online may be growing in the teens as an estimate.

2.  **Google Pay**: “Over 2.1B transactions were processed by Google Pay in 2025, showing a 30% increase from the previous year.”[1](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-1-186769835) Not directly comparable to TPV but signals it is meaningfully more growth than 6%.

3.  **Amazon Pay**: “Amazon Pay processed an estimated $95B in transactions, marking an 11.8% increase compared to 2024.”​[2](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-2-186769835)

4.  **Shop Pay**: In Q1-Q3 gross merchandise volume through Shop Pay has been growing above 60%. Not a market wide metric but shows that inside Shopify they are taking share.

5.  **Klarna**: As of Q3 2025 GMV was up 23% year over year to $32.7B which was accelerating from earlier in the year.[3](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-3-186769835)

6.  **Affirm**: As of Q4 2025 revenue was up 33% YoY.


This is what investors are seeing. Competition coming from everywhere and, outside of BNPL and Venmo, taking ground.

I know what you are thinking. Looking at this data, you might think PayPal is finished. But there's a critical distinction between losing share and collapsing entirely. I phrased my question intentionally.

_Can PayPal’s branded checkout avoid collapsing?_

Today PayPal trades at an EV/FCF of less than 7x. Their median since IPO is above 20x.

Markets are pricing in a melting ice cube led by their branded checkout. So what could stop that from happening?

Branded checkout success is based on two things.

1.  Ease of Use

2.  Trust


Historically and to this day, PayPal is one of the most trusted names in payments. For many years they were the only real game in town. But things are changing and competition is heating up.

To put this in perspective, PayPal launched One Touch in 2015, letting users check out with one touch and skip logins. Three years later they added Smart Buttons for faster checkout. Then nearly nothing until 2024. Nine years of minimal innovation.

During that same period, Apple, Google, Amazon, Stripe, and Adyen were rapidly iterating. PayPal fell behind on ease of use while competitors caught up on trust.

For years, PayPal had little incentive to innovate. They were the most trusted name by far, and trust matters more than ease of use. That worked fine until new competition emerged.

As commerce moved online during COVID, these platforms gained share. The trend was already forming, but the pandemic accelerated it.

Revenue growth decelerated after 2020 and 2021. Not because customers stopped trusting PayPal, but because there were now multiple trusted options that were as easy or easier to use.

But the story isn't over. Look at unbranded checkout and peer-to-peer. Both troughed then accelerated on new innovation over the last two years.

PayPal knows how to innovate. Rolling out improvements in branded checkout is just taking longer.

CEO Alex Chriss had this to say at the end of 2025 at the Citi Fintech conference:

_“Only 20% complete after significant time. That’s probably the piece I underestimated the most in terms of just how long it would take to get that experience out to customers.”_

So what are those changes and how will they keep PayPal from becoming a melting ice cube, at least for now?

* * *

**Fastlane** (Mid 2024)

Fastlane is an accelerated guest checkout experience. Shoppers opt in once, PayPal stores their shipping and payment details, and on future Fastlane merchants they can check out in as little as one click.

Aggregated internal data for April 3 to June 15, 2024 shows Fastlane shoppers completing guest checkout roughly 32% faster and achieving around 80% conversion versus 74% for non-accelerated guest shoppers.[4](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-4-186769835)

_Assessment: Matching Competition_

**Biometrics and Passkeys (**2024**)**

A redesigned login and checkout that integrates passkeys and biometric authentication (face/fingerprint), so many users can approve payments with a single tap instead of entering a password plus SMS code.

_Assessment: Matching Competition_

**Agentic Commerce** (2025)

PayPal announced an “agentic commerce” suite designed to let AI agents (like Perplexity and other assistants) discover products and complete purchases on merchants’ behalf using PayPal rails.

AI is already embedded in day-to-day financial services operations. BCG estimates that 81% of consumers expect to shop using AI-powered purchasing agents within two years, representing over $1 trillion in agent-assisted e-commerce spend.[5](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-5-186769835)

_Assessment: Early and Beating Competition_

Going down about 5% relative decline per year since 2012 but slowing to a 2.5% relative decline per year over the last three years.

Within PayPal, branded checkout still earns several times the net take rate of lower‑margin unbranded enterprise processing, so as Braintree and other low‑take‑rate flows grow faster, each dollar of TPV is worth a bit less unless VAS and pricing improvements offset it.

Altogether I believe that PayPal will be able to avoid any significant deterioration of their branded checkout, but it may still come with pain.

Do not count out their ability to continue to grow.

If they can roll out recent innovations more quickly we could see a reacceleration in branded checkout numbers over time. It will not be massive double-digit growth, but even small growth from their main profit driver will mean big things for the company.

_Now the question is, can they grow in other areas profitably?_

Profitable Growth


===================

First I had to ask, are these companies competing in a race to the bottom?

The answer over the last 10 years is a definitive no. With the last three continuing the trend of margin expansion.

So what will the future bring?

With competition rising, cost excellence has become a growth lever. BCG finds that payments providers deploying AI routing, smarter authorization, and leaner operations achieve 30%+ stronger margins.[6](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-6-186769835)

And there is another layer we have yet to discuss that I believe will be key for the future of payments, and margins, without needing a crystal ball.

AI has made it clear to the world data is power. And in payments, data means better fraud/risk protection, more targeted advertising, and clear real-time performance metrics.

This is known as value-added services. These payment providers don’t just process your payments. They package all the data they are collecting in the form of tools and sell it back to you so you can make better informed, quicker decisions.

And even better, they use the data themselves to improve authorization rates, which reduces denials.

The king of data in online payments is PayPal and it isn’t even close.

[

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!S9qQ!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8dd69493-7a8a-4895-86e2-1ff433b774b8_1600x1600.png)

That means the quality and quantity of data they have to monetize far exceeds their competitors. And this isn't something competitors can replicate quickly - you can't manufacture 25 years of transaction history across 400M+ users. The data moat is real.

And after growth here stagnated in 2024, they are back to double-digit growth over 14% in 2025.

[

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!AaD0!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa942e31a-ec73-4474-8840-d6691a591b2b_1600x1600.png)

And they have a lot more monetization and growth runway outside of agentic commerce, which on its own could be massive.

*   In 2024 PayPal launched AI-powered advertising using transaction data from 400M+ users and 26B annual transactions. The platform leverages 25 years of purchase history data. A dataset larger than Amazon or Walmart's, according to PayPal.

    *   Advertisers include Mercedes-Benz, Uber, DoorDash, and Walmart with analysts anticipating ads alone could generate $150-250M of new revenue in 2026 alone.

*   In 2026 PayPal launched Transaction Graph Insights, allowing insights into cross-merchant purchase data for ad targeting.


CEO Alex Chriss described VAS as a _"massive untapped and margin-rich opportunity."_

So far I have only touched on the exciting innovative things but there is even more happening.

*   Peer to Peer Venmo is growing double-digit with $1.7B in revenue. Still has a long runway and is gaining share in this multi-trillion TPV market.

*   Debit cards at Venmo grew 40% in 2025 and users with debit cards generate 2x the ARPU of online-only users.

*   Buy-Now-Pay-Later grew 20% in 2025.

*   PYUSD circulation grew over 600% in 2025. As it scales, it can reduce settlement costs on some flows by letting them bypass parts of traditional bank or card systems.


So yes, I think it is very likely PayPal can continue to grow, but more importantly do so profitably.

These high growth areas make up a small part of revenue and profits today but will continue to grow to be a larger piece of the pie. And they will help profitability as the high margins on branded checkout continue to fall.

They could have stagnating or even negative growth in the near term like guidance suggests, but they have significant positive momentum outside of legacy branded checkout.

The only thing left to do is for the new CEO to execute. _(Luckily Alex Chriss did most of the hard work already.)_

And the timing here matters. Sentiment is completely flushed out after years of declines. Expectations couldn't be lower. Which means a new CEO with a track record of execution and aggressive capital return has an incredibly low bar to clear. This is the setup where positive surprises compound quickly.

Management


============

As a bit of a shock to investors in PayPal’s Q4 2025 conference call, they announced Enrique Lores would be taking over as CEO effective immediately.

Previously under Alex Chriss who came from Intuit, the board said it was mainly due to the slow pace of change that he was replaced.

The changes and innovation under his leadership were the most important steps needed to turn the company around. Number of users returned to growth, returns on capital improved, and both topline and operating income hit records each year.

In the end though, he is gone now, so let’s see what Enrique can offer PayPal.

Before this change, he spent 36 years at HP, becoming President and CEO in 2019, only leaving for this new position.

While there, Enrique was known for cutting costs and returning capital to shareholders aggressively.

Share count fell 35% and he returned almost $23B to shareholders via dividends and buybacks in the 6 years he led there. That aggressive policy led to returns of over 150% from start to peak, though after hitting that peak in 2022 the stock has trended down.

While those returns are good, understanding the business he inherited makes them more interesting. In 2019 HP was roughly 1/3 printing and 2/3 computers.

Printing is a structurally declining business and computers, apart from the boom during Covid, were going through a cycle trough. And even still, with those headwinds he was able to generate shareholder returns.

Here at PayPal, which is a better business, I think we can expect better results but achieved through very similar methods.

It could be a blazing first couple quarters with big announcements and job cuts.

A secondary but still worth noting piece of information is before he really took the reins, guidance was set very low. Which means we could avoid the typical first quarter under a new CEO of big negative news to create a low bar. Especially since he has been on the board for roughly 5 years and should be familiar enough with the company to get started right away.

For 2026 here is what guidance they gave:

*   Transaction margin dollars (the gross profit of transaction revenues): slightly down YoY or excluding interest income flat YoY

*   Non-GAAP EPS: “low single digit decline to slightly positive growth”

*   Adjusted FCF of $6B+

*   $6B of share repurchases (almost 16% of market cap)

*   $1B of Capex


Impressively while at HP, Enrique had 15 consecutive quarters of guidance meets or beats. Impressive for a cyclical hardware company.

And led me to believe he may have had a part in why guidance was so low. He wants to come out beating expectations right away. And setting expectations at rock bottom is the easiest way to do that.

Not everything was fantastic under his leadership though.

*   Stock based compensation rose 75%.

*   Revenue fell ~6%.

*   Debt rose ~85%. (albeit from low levels, always staying below 2.2x debt to EBITDA)

*   Median FCF margins fell over 1% vs the prior 6 year median.


Doesn’t sound as great when you look at the whole picture, but again I think understanding the business he was running makes the picture more accurate.

> The business was in structural and cyclical decline and he was still able to generate meaningful shareholder returns.
>
> To me that is a sign of a manager willing to do what it takes.

And this is what he has said about what PayPal needs to do:

“_We will further strengthen the culture of innovation necessary to deliver long-term transformation and balance this with near-term delivery, executing with greater speed and precision, and holding ourselves accountable for consistent delivery quarter on quarter._”

“_The payments industry is changing faster than ever, driven by new technologies, evolving regulations, an increasingly competitive landscape, and the rapid acceleration of AI that is reshaping commerce daily. PayPal sits at the center of this change._”

“_I think PayPal has a great future. We need to improve execution and advance our ongoing initiatives._”

He highlighted the following priorities in an interview with Yahoo Finance:

*   Maintaining momentum in Venmo and Buy Now, Pay Later.

*   Boosting performance in branded checkout.

*   Focus on growth, investment, and improving execution, not just cutting costs.


The points he is hitting on are exactly what PayPal needs to succeed long term. And seeing progress made and capital returned to shareholders is what will improve confidence in the short term.

All of which I believe Enrique is capable of, not because he is the best in the world at innovation, but because he seems to be a good executor.

That ability to execute though is also the biggest risk.

Execution and Risks


=====================

It should be made very clear PayPal has been late to innovation for years, only stepping up their game in 2024.

If Enrique is unable to roll out the new features quickly and capitalize on new areas of growth like VAS, Venmo, BNPL, and Agentic Commerce, PayPal could turn into the melting ice cube the market fears.

Competition is fierce and PayPal will likely continue to lose share. I am not suggesting otherwise. Only that they can do far better than the incredibly low expectations the market has set for them.

And keep in mind, losing share does not mean chronically shrinking profits or topline. This is a growing industry and PayPal will benefit directly from that natural growth.

The global digital payments market is projected to grow at roughly 9-10% annually through 2030, with e-commerce payment value rising from $8.3T in 2025 to $13T by 2030.[7](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-7-186769835) The payment gateway segment (where Braintree and PayPal’s unbranded processing compete) is forecast to grow revenue at about 22% annually over the same period.[8](https://deepvaluecapitalbykyler.substack.com/p/dv-capital-paypal-no-pain-no-gain#footnote-8-186769835)

Even if PayPal loses a few points of market share, they’re losing share in a pie growing double digits. That's the math working in their favor.

A few other areas worth mentioning:

*   **Recession**: Would hurt all payment processors so it is not a risk worth highlighting in detail. Especially when I cannot predict them.

*   **Income from Interest:** Already built into the small declines in 2026 and after that will likely be a more stable comparable after a year of rate cuts in 2025.

*   **BNPL Regulation:** Risk has come down after the recent rules classifying BNPL digital accounts as “credit cards” was revoked, but that does not mean it is immune from future changes. Especially on the state level, this is more likely to happen with 17 states issuing BNPL regulation proposals in 2025, mainly around transparency and some caps on fees.

*   **Stablecoins:** An area I know relatively little about and in fact only studied first here learning about PYUSD. And while their use case seems straightforward and that it could be a boost to margins, I could be missing something material.

*   **Cybersecurity Breaches:** Becoming more common and while I am sure PayPal is taking extensive measures to protect information, it could erode trust (their most important asset) if there was an incident.

*   **Potential Sale:** Enrique could pursue a sale of PayPal at these low valuations, capping upside potential.

So with that said, let’s get down to the question of what PayPal is really worth.

#### Revenue

Topline for PayPal in the near term is unlikely to have any major growth until BNPL, Venmo, and VAS grow to a bigger portion of the pie. My base case assumes low single digit positive growth for 2026, accelerating into 2027 and the first half of 2028, reaching mid to high single digits.

This growth will be hindered by branded checkout, especially in the first half of 2026, until the rollout of innovations can really move forward. After that is done though, I expect branded checkout to stabilize while other areas continue to perform well.

1H 2028 TTM Revenue: $37.66B

This does assume TPV growing mid single digits in 2026, accelerating to low double digits by 2028.

#### Margins

Forecasting PayPal’s margins has puts and takes. Take rates will continue declining against a largely fixed cost base, but high margin VAS revenue will likely keep climbing. Given the weight of branded checkout and expected higher Capex, I believe a slight decline in FCF margins is warranted.

Over the last 4 years, FCF margins have averaged 17.7%.

1H 2028 TTM FCF Margin: 16.5%

1H 2028 FCF: $6.21B

#### Multiple

Forecasting a multiple is always incredibly difficult given how closely tied to short term sentiment it can be. In the end though, for a company growing mid single digits with double digit returns on capital, I would typically assign a higher than market average multiple.

The take though is that AI and industry change, like we are seeing with software, could compress investors’ expectations and willingness to pay a high multiple. In addition PayPal pays a large portion of FCF as SBC.

Base Case Multiple: 16x FCF (Excluding SBC 20x)

PayPal will likely still have many years of runway if they are able to do well in Agentic commerce. And given they are an early mover with high trust, I do think that is likely and could easily drive a higher multiple.

As another point of reference, other companies in payments trade well above 20x and even 30x FCF historically. They generally do have higher growth, but that gives you a comparison for how conservative this is generally.

#### Share Count

As of Q4 2025, PayPal had 920.7M shares outstanding. They are pursuing significant share repurchases that will affect the terminal value here. I believe it is reasonable we could see 12% share count reduction in 2026, 9% in 2027, and 6% in 2028 depending on share price.
