Here is a breakdown of the information regarding purchase value:

### I. Recommended Minimum Value Thresholds

To screen for trades that signal genuine conviction, you should filter out transactions that are too small to be meaningful:

*   **Recommended Minimum:** Focus on purchases of **\$200,000 or more**.
*   **Initial Cutoff:** While some suggest a minimum of **\$100,000**, \$200,000 is often preferred as a higher threshold for significance.
*   **Third-Party Cutoff:** Some third-party sites use a minimum cutoff of greater than **\$25,000** to eliminate small figures, but this amount is generally considered **too low** to demonstrate significant conviction.
*   **Ignoring Insignificant Amounts:** Transactions of very small figures, such as \$95, \$1,000, \$4,000, or even \$5,000 to \$15,000, are viewed as "not important," "not useful," or routine 401k contributions and should be ignored. If an insider is putting \$3,000–\$15,000 into the stock, it is unlikely to be opportunistic buying.

### II. Relative Significance and Conviction

The absolute dollar value must be evaluated relative to the insider's financial context to assess the true level of conviction ("skin in the game").

| Contextual Criteria | Rationale and Sources |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Relative Position Size** | Look for trades that represent a significant increase in the insider’s current holdings, such as **20% to 50%**. A large shareholder increasing their stake by only 0.5% (like a purchase of \$3 million by a billionaire who owns 18.3 million shares) shows low conviction, regardless of the large dollar size. Insiders are expected to put **"a lot of skin in the game"**. |
| **Relative to Wealth/Salary** | Consider the purchase size relative to the insider's annual salary or net worth. An executive buying stock equivalent to their entire annual salary signals high conviction. Conversely, a multi-million dollar purchase by a billionaire might be viewed as insignificant if it's a tiny fraction of their overall wealth. |
| **Trade Type** | Open market purchases (where prices are **non-uniform** or varied) are preferred over participation in new share offerings, where the purchase may be mandatory or required by other investors to ensure management has "more skin in the game," making the rationale harder to interpret. |

### III. Using Tools to Filter by Value

Screening tools are essential for implementing these filters efficiently:

*   **Scanning by Value:** When using screening platforms (like GuruFocus), it is better to scan by the **total dollar value** of shares bought rather than the number of shares. This is because the price of shares varies widely, making the share number an unreliable indicator of investment size.
*   **Implementing Filters:** You can set the minimum value filter on tools like the GuruFocus All-in-One Screener to **\$200,000 or higher** to narrow down candidates.
*   **Avoiding Option Exercises:** Always filter out transactions that involve the exercise of derivative securities (like stock options, typically coded 'M' on Form 4s), as these often look like a buy but are compensation being cashed out, regardless of the dollar size.

In summary, the value of a purchase is a critical factor, but it must be assessed in conjunction with other qualitative factors (like the insider's position and whether the purchase is part of a cluster buy) and relative metrics (like percentage increase in holdings) to determine if it truly represents an **opportunistic, high-conviction signal**.
