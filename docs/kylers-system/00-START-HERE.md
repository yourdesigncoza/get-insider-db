He got bitten by the investing bug at 15 when his uncle made an offhand comment about his investments. Kyler asked a few follow-up questions, went down the rabbit hole, and never came back up. By 16, he convinced his parents to open a brokerage account and let him manage it.

He had no idea what he was doing -- and he paid the price.

He chased trends. Bought hype. Blew up his account more than once trying to "get rich" fast. One week, after spending just 5-10 hours learning about options, he bought a call option that doubled his money. Feeling like a genius, he went bigger -- bought a Boeing call option expiring the next day. It soared 200%+ in hours. The next morning, Boeing opened down and that position expired worthless.

> **Key Idea:** Those painful early losses were the best thing that could have happened to Kyler. They forced him to actually learn the craft. He started reading and watching everything he could -- and that path led him to deep value and turnaround investing.

Outside of family and the occasional round of golf, writing, researching, and investing have been his obsessions for years. He wakes up early to write and research before work. During the day, he's listening to books, earnings calls, macro context, or stock pitches. He recently quit his full-time job to invest and write full time.

## What Is DeepValue Capital?

DeepValue Capital is Kyler's Substack newsletter and personal portfolio. It's not a hedge fund or a financial advisory -- it's his own money, his own research, published for subscribers to learn from.

He specializes in **turnaround investing** -- finding quality businesses the market has written off because of temporary issues. In those situations, prices often reflect fear rather than **fundamentals** (the actual financial health and performance of the business -- see [09-GLOSSARY.md](09-GLOSSARY.md)).

> **Key Idea:** "The stock and business are not the same thing. In public markets you are often presented the opportunity to buy a dollar for loose change."

Think of it this way. Imagine a great restaurant in your town that had a kitchen fire. It's closed for repairs. The landlord panics and lists the building for sale at half price because the restaurant "isn't making money." But you know the restaurant was packed every night before the fire, has loyal customers, and the repairs will be done in three months. You'd buy that building in a heartbeat. That's what Kyler does -- but with stocks instead of buildings.

### How He Invests

- **Concentrated portfolio**: Maximum of 12 positions at any time. He doesn't spread money across hundreds of stocks -- he puts meaningful amounts into his best ideas.
- **Hold period**: Typically 1-4 years as the underlying problems resolve.
- **Risk first**: Downside risk and **conviction** (how confident he is in the idea, backed by research -- see [09-GLOSSARY.md](09-GLOSSARY.md)) carry the most weight in every decision.
- **Patience is part of the edge**: While he tracks many ideas, only a few become actual portfolio positions each year. Inactivity is normal.

> **Key Idea:** This is not a short-term trading approach. It's not designed for day traders, swing traders, or anyone looking for quick wins. It's built for investors willing to do the work and hold through volatility.

## What "Deep Value Turnaround Investing" Means

Let's break this term into pieces:

- **Deep Value**: Buying something for far less than it's actually worth. Not just "a little cheap" -- dramatically underpriced.
- **Turnaround**: A company going through hard times that is actively working to fix its problems and return to health.

Put them together, and you get Kyler's specialty: finding quality businesses that are temporarily beaten down, where the stock price reflects the fear and bad news, but the underlying business is stronger than the market believes.

**The House Analogy**

Imagine you're looking at houses in a great neighborhood. One house had a burst pipe that flooded the basement. The owners panicked and listed it at 50% off. Most buyers see "water damage" and run away. But you check the foundation -- it's solid. The plumbing company says repairs will cost $5,000 and take two weeks. The house is worth $400,000 in that neighborhood, and it's listed at $200,000 because of a fixable problem.

That's deep value turnaround investing. You're buying quality at a panic-driven discount, waiting for the temporary problem to resolve, and then the value becomes obvious to everyone else.

Kyler avoids what's popular, what has already gone up in price, and situations that fall outside his understanding or have a history of destroying investor money.

## Core Principles to Keep in Mind

Before you dive in, here are the beliefs that run through everything Kyler does:

1. **The stock and business are not the same thing.** Stock prices bounce around based on fear, greed, and headlines. The business underneath keeps running.

2. **In public markets, you are often presented the opportunity to buy a dollar for loose change.** When the market panics, prices disconnect from reality. That's where the opportunity lives.

3. **Discipline beats talent.** Having a system and sticking to it matters more than being the smartest person in the room.

4. **Risk management comes first.** Every step in the process is designed to maximize returns *while managing risk*. The goal is to find companies that are not just cheap, but fundamentally sound and protected on the downside.

5. **Patience is an edge.** Kyler is willing to hold cash if he can't find attractive opportunities. He doesn't force trades.

6. **Stay in your circle of competence.** Only invest in industries you understand. It's better to know one area deeply than to spread yourself thin across everything.

> **Key Idea:** "Conviction doesn't come from having a high return estimate in a spreadsheet. It comes from understanding how the real business is evolving over time."
